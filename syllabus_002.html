Course Description
We study decision-making in dynamic environments under uncertainty. Topics include optimization techniques, recursive and hyperbolic preferences, intertemporal consumption, general equilibrium in complete and incomplete markets, search theory, repeated and markovian games. Emphasis is placed on model building, presentations, and discussions in support of the individual research project.
Learning Objectives

The course main goal is to provide tools to enable you to:

  • Construct models of micro-economic behavior in dynamic environments
  • Identify their (built-in) limitations
  • Think critically about how to apply them to real-life

We will also write a research essay/proposal/project aimed at the learning outcomes described by the IDEAS in action (Research) curricula.

General AI Guidelines
  1. AI should help you think, not think for you. You may be able to use these tools to brainstorm ideas, research topics, and analyze problems, but you must decide what's appropriate and accurate.
  2. Engage responsibly with AI. You must evaluate AI-generated outputs for potential biases, limitations, inaccuracies, false output, and ethical implications. Do not put personal or confidential data into these tools.

ECON 510 Additional AI Guidelines
  • AI assistance is permitted for homework and projects only. AI use is not allowed during exams or quizzes.
