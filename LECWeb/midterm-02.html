Part I — Public Good Provision

Set-up

Sequential public good provision

Three individuals $i=1,2,3$ contribute sequentially to the public good $G=g_1+g_2+g_3$. In the last round, individual $3$ chooses $g_3$ to maximize $$\sqrt{w_3-g_3}+\sqrt{g_1+g_2+g_3}\quad\st\quad 0\leq g_3\leq w_3.$$ Here $w_1$, $w_2$, $w_3$, $g_1$, and $g_2$ are taken as given when individual $3$ moves.

Question 1

Economic meaning of $0\leq g_3$

Question: Explain the economic meaning of the constraint $0\leq g_3$.

Answer

Individual $3$ can only contribute resources to the public good; they cannot withdraw from it. A negative $g_3$ would mean appropriating part of the public good for private use, which is not allowed. The constraint $g_3\geq 0$ rules out such expropriation and enforces a non-negativity (only-give) restriction on contributions.

Question 2

Economic meaning of $g_3\leq w_3$

Question: Explain the economic meaning of the constraint $g_3\leq w_3$.

Answer

Individual $3$ cannot contribute more than they own. The endowment $w_3$ is the upper bound on what can be given away, since the private consumption $x_3=w_3-g_3$ must remain non-negative. In short, $g_3\leq w_3$ is the budget feasibility (no-borrowing) constraint.

Question 4

The Lagrangian

Question: Write the Lagrangian for individual $3$'s problem — substituting the actual objective and constraints.

Answer

Writing both inequality constraints in the form $g_i(x)\leq c_i$: $$-g_3\leq 0\quad\text{and}\quad g_3\leq w_3,$$ and using the rule $-\lambda_i\cdot(g_i(x)-c_i)$ for each one: $$\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}(g_3,\lambda_1,\lambda_2)&=\sqrt{w_3-g_3}+\sqrt{g_1+g_2+g_3}-\lambda_1\cdot(-g_3-0)-\lambda_2\cdot(g_3-w_3)\\ &=\sqrt{w_3-g_3}+\sqrt{g_1+g_2+g_3}+\lambda_1\cdot g_3+\lambda_2\cdot(w_3-g_3). \end{aligned}$$

Question 5

First-order conditions when $\lambda_1\gt 0$, $\lambda_2=0$

Question: Assume $\lambda_1\gt 0$ and $\lambda_2=0$. Write all first-order conditions and simplify.

Answer

Replacing $\lambda_2=0$ in the (simplified) Lagrangian, we apply Recipe #1 to the variables $g_3$ and $\lambda_1$. There are two FOCs.

FOC w.r.t. $g_3$: $$\diff{\mathcal{L}}{g_3}=-\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{w_3-g_3}}+\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{g_1+g_2+g_3}}+\lambda_1=0.$$

FOC w.r.t. $\lambda_1$: $$\diff{\mathcal{L}}{\lambda_1}=g_3=0.$$

Question 6

Solving the FOCs

Question: Solve the FOCs from Question 5. Your answer may depend on $w_3$, $g_1$, and $g_2$.

Answer

The FOC w.r.t. $\lambda_1$ gives $g_3=0$. Substituting $g_3=0$ into the FOC w.r.t. $g_3$ and solving for $\lambda_1$: $$\boxed{g_3=0,\qquad \lambda_1=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{w_3}}-\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{g_1+g_2}}.}$$

Economically: at this candidate solution, individual $3$ is a free-rider — they contribute nothing and consume their entire endowment privately ($x_3=w_3$).

Question 7

When is the candidate a true solution?

Question: What conditions on $w_3$, $g_1$, $g_2$ ensure that the candidate found in Question 6 is actually a solution to the maximization problem?

Answer

We need the multiplier $\lambda_1$ to be (strictly) positive, as assumed in the case: $$\lambda_1=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{w_3}}-\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{g_1+g_2}}\gt 0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\sqrt{g_1+g_2}\gt\sqrt{w_3}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\boxed{g_1+g_2\gt w_3.}$$

Interpretation: if the public good is already large enough relative to individual $3$'s wealth, the marginal utility of one more unit of $G$ falls below the marginal utility of private consumption, so it is optimal to free-ride ($g_3=0$).

Part II — Contingent Markets

Set-up

Two-period economy

Each individual has utility $u(c_0,c_1)=c_0^{\alpha}+\delta\cdot c_1^{\alpha}$ and budget $c_0+p\cdot c_1=\text{income}$. The individual demands are $$c_0=\dfrac{K\cdot\text{income}}{K+p},\qquad c_1=\dfrac{\text{income}}{K+p},\qquad K=\paren{\delta/p}^{1/(\alpha-1)}.$$ Endowments: $e_i=(5i,\,20-5i)$ for $i=1,2,3$.

Question 8

Endowments and incomes

Question: (1) Write the numerical endowment for each individual. (2) Write each individual's income as a function of $p$ only.

Answer

Endowments: $$e_1=(5,15),\qquad e_2=(10,10),\qquad e_3=(15,5).$$

Incomes (value of endowment at prices $(1,p)$): $\text{income}_i=5i+p\cdot(20-5i)$, so $$\text{income}_1=5+15p,\quad \text{income}_2=10+10p,\quad \text{income}_3=15+5p.$$

Question 10

Total demand for $c_0$

Question: Write the total demand for consumption today, $c_0$, in terms of $p$ and $K$.

Answer

Sum the individual demands $c_{0,i}=K\cdot\text{income}_i/(K+p)$: $$C_0=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\dfrac{K\cdot\text{income}_i}{K+p}=\dfrac{K}{K+p}\bracket{(5+15p)+(10+10p)+(15+5p)}.$$ The bracket equals $30+30p=30(1+p)$, hence $$\boxed{C_0(p)=\dfrac{30\,K\,(1+p)}{K+p}.}$$

Question 11

Equilibrium price equation

Question: Write the equation that must be solved to find the equilibrium $p$.

Answer

Total endowment of $c_0$ equals $5+10+15=30$. Market clearing for the period-$0$ good requires total demand equal to total supply: $$\dfrac{30\,K\,(1+p)}{K+p}=30\;\Longleftrightarrow\;K(1+p)=K+p\;\Longleftrightarrow\;K=1.$$ Since $K=\paren{\delta/p}^{1/(\alpha-1)}$, the condition $K=1$ gives $\delta/p=1$, i.e. $$\boxed{p^{e}=\delta.}$$ (One could equivalently use market clearing for $c_1$ — since each individual's budget binds, clearing one market automatically clears the other, so both routes yield the same equation.)

Part III — Spot & Financial Markets

Set-up

Bond markets replace contingent markets

Same preferences and endowments as Part II. There is a single bond issued at $t=0$: each unit costs $1$ in period $0$ and pays $R$ in period $1$. Let $z$ denote the amount of the bond purchased (with $z\lt 0$ meaning the agent borrows).

Question 12

Period-by-period budget for individual 1

Question: Individual 1 has endowment $e_1=(5,15)$. (1) Write the period-$0$ budget. (2) Write the period-$1$ budget.

Answer

Period $t=0$: spend on consumption today and on bonds, using the period-$0$ endowment: $$c_0+z=5.$$

Period $t=1$: consume the period-$1$ endowment plus the bond payoff: $$c_1=15+R\cdot z.$$

Question 13

From period budgets to a single intertemporal constraint

Question: (1) Solve the period-$1$ budget for $z$. (2) Substitute into the period-$0$ budget and place all consumption on the LHS. (3) Compare with the contingent-markets budget $c_0+p\cdot c_1=5+15p$.

Answer

(1) From $c_1=15+Rz$: $$z=\dfrac{c_1-15}{R}.$$

(2) Substituting into $c_0+z=5$ and rearranging: $$c_0+\dfrac{c_1-15}{R}=5\;\Longrightarrow\;\boxed{c_0+\dfrac{1}{R}\,c_1=5+\dfrac{15}{R}.}$$

(3) Compared with $c_0+p\cdot c_1=5+15p$, the two budgets coincide whenever $$p=\dfrac{1}{R}\quad\paren{\text{equivalently, }R=\dfrac{1}{p}}.$$ The bond economy and the contingent-markets economy generate the same set of feasible $(c_0,c_1)$ bundles — the gross interest rate $R$ is the reciprocal of the contingent price $p$.
